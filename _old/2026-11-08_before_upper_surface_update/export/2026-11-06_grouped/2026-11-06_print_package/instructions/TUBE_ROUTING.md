# Tube routing: A1–D3

Two fixed networks, each with six cells. G1: A1, C1, B2, D2, A3, C3. G2: B1, D1, A2, C2, B3, D3. Row 1 is nearest the service bay. One supported bottom port and one bidirectional branch serve each bladder.

Tube allowance: 4 mm ID, 6 mm OD, assumed minimum centreline bend radius 12 mm. These require an actual hose trial. Each nominal 6 mm stem is covered by a straight hose insertion, with additional straight drop on G1 before the bend. Cell branches turn through two R12 bends toward separate branch lanes, G1 at row-centre Y+24/z10 and G2 at Y−24/z20. Three four-way crosses on each trunk join the two cell branches at each row; its final unused nipple is plugged. The trunks are at X−20 and X+20 and retained by six screw-mounted clips.

Each group tee connects its fill and extraction valve outlets to the trunk. Four NC 2/2 valves separate the groups from common pressure and suction headers. Pumps within each bank share control; pump nozzle positions, extraction compatibility and bank isolation remain unverified. The two groups must not share an uncontrolled tee.

Actual 3D OD envelopes, fitting keep-outs and frame passages are included in the CAD and [routing image](../export/2026-11-06_grouped/2026-11-06_routing.png). The [cut list](../export/2026-11-06_grouped/2026-11-06_hose_cut_list.csv) contains centreline lengths, not production cuts. Add fitting insertion and practical removal allowance after the trial; no arbitrary extra hose is proven to fit the enclosure.

Reference barbs intentionally overlap the hose ID elastic-fit region; fitting dimensions and hose stretch must be verified. Bladder sealing uses a supported weld-compatible fitting, rather than relying on printed FDM walls as an airtight nozzle. Check both networks for leaks, pinching, unintended backflow and proper closed-valve hold before operation.
